Product definition

Connected Envelope Budget

A bank-connected budgeting product that turns new credit-card activity into an interactive text conversation, helping users consciously place every purchase into a persistent envelope.

Public launch Monday, September 16, 2026. Limited release: 5-7 test users + early customers.

01 / Intent

Make every purchase visible and intentional

The product replaces manual spreadsheet entry with a guided envelope-budgeting experience. It connects to credit-card accounts, detects recent charges, cleans up bank descriptions, and asks the user by text where each charge belongs.

Problem today

Bank alerts provide awareness, but they do not connect purchases to a budget. The user must read each alert and manually re-enter it into spreadsheet categories.

Desired outcome

The app becomes the interactive notification layer: it reports the purchase, proposes a category, accepts a reply, updates the envelope, and warns when spending approaches or exceeds the plan.

Product principle

The application may assist with merchant cleanup and category suggestions, but the user remains physically involved in categorizing purchases. It should create a small moment of spending awareness rather than making budgeting invisible.

Initial audience: designed from a single-user use case, but account creation, templates, bank connections, and configurable categories make the concept suitable for additional individual users later.

02 / Primary workflow

The app sends the charge alert

01User securely connects a supported credit-card account.
02Bank-data provider reports a new charge to the application.
03App cleans the merchant name and suggests a category by text.
04User confirms or chooses another category; envelope updates.

The first bank-connected version supports credit cards only. The app notifies as soon as the provider reports a transaction. The user accepts that these notifications may arrive later than native bank alerts because aggregator timing differs by issuer.

Timing reality

A connected-bank provider does not guarantee real-time delivery. “As soon as available” may still mean minutes, hours, or occasionally longer after purchase. This is a product constraint that must be communicated honestly rather than presented as identical to native bank alerts.

03 / Guided onboarding

Start with a useful structure, not a blank screen

Account setup should reduce decision fatigue while keeping every recommendation editable.

  1. Create credentials using username and password.
  2. Enter the monthly take-home amount available for budgeting.
  3. Choose a category template that creates starter envelopes.
  4. Choose an allocation model, beginning with standard 50/30/20.
  5. Review suggested category dollar amounts calculated from the budget.
  6. Rename, add, archive, and adjust envelopes before confirming setup.
  7. Connect one or more supported credit-card accounts.
  8. Confirm the mobile number used for interactive notifications.
Category template

Defines what the user budgets

Creates starter categories such as housing, groceries, dining, transportation, insurance, personal spending, emergency savings, and long-term savings.

Allocation model

Defines how the budget is divided

50% Needs, 30% Wants, and 20% Savings is the initial model. Users may customize the percentages and then adjust every suggested dollar amount.

Templates provide starter categories and suggested amounts. They do not lock the user into fixed names or allocations. The template and allocation model are separate choices: first choose the category structure, then choose how the monthly total should be divided.

04 / Envelope model

Balances persist across months

Each active category has a monthly contribution. The user explicitly closes the prior month before the next contribution is added. Both surplus and overspending roll forward.

Next envelope balance
prior balance + monthly contribution - ordinary spending - scheduled spread installments

Category targets may total more than the monthly budgeting amount. The interface must visibly warn about over-allocation but must not prevent the user from saving it.

Category lifecycle

  • Create and rename categories.
  • Change monthly contributions.
  • Set near-limit warning thresholds.
  • Archive categories while preserving history.

Historical corrections

  • Move transactions between categories.
  • Record changes in an audit trail.
  • Recalculate affected months forward.
  • Preserve the original bank record.
05 / Spread spending

Large charges can affect several future budget periods

The user may spread a transaction such as a six-month auto-insurance payment across a chosen number of months. The first installment counts in the purchase month.

Example
$600 insurance charge ÷ 6 months = $100 budget impact in the purchase month + $100 in each of the next 5 months

The user may later change the duration, causing remaining installments to be recalculated. The original transaction remains a single `$600` credit-card charge.

Required accounting distinction

The selected behavior deducts only `$100` from the envelope each month even though the credit-card balance increased by `$600` immediately. This intentionally smooths budget impact, but it can make the envelope appear to contain money that is already owed to the card issuer. The product must always show both values: actual charge: $600 and current-month budget impact: $100. It must never rewrite the bank transaction as six separate purchases.

This feature should be named “Spread budget impact” or similar, not “payment plan,” because it does not change what is owed to the credit-card company.

06 / Transaction records

Clean at a glance, complete on demand

The transaction list should prioritize scanning. Each row displays only:

DateAmountClean merchant name

Opening a transaction reveals its raw bank description, account or card nickname, pending or posted status, notes, original amount, category, spread schedule when applicable, and edit history.

Claude provides merchant-name suggestions using the minimum necessary context. It must not receive the user's name, full card details, bank identifiers, or raw provider metadata. The cleaned name is presentation metadata, not the financial source of truth. Transaction amount and identity always come from the bank-data provider.

07 / AI categorization assistant

Turn an uncategorized backlog into a guided conversation

Users are not required to answer every text. The website provides a Claude-powered assistant that reviews uncategorized charges in batches, explains its suggestions in natural language, and keeps the user in control of every final assignment.

You have 36 uncategorized charges. I grouped 6 as Groceries, 10 as Shopping, and 4 as Miscellaneous. I am less certain about 4 other charges. Would you like to review my suggestions?
The grocery group looks right, but let me review Shopping.
I selected the 10 Shopping suggestions in the review workspace. Deselect any that do not belong, then approve the rest together.

Conversation panel

  • Summarizes the uncategorized backlog.
  • Explains proposed category groups.
  • Asks focused questions about uncertain charges.
  • Understands corrections in natural language.

Review workspace

  • Displays selectable charges grouped by suggestion.
  • Allows incorrect items to be deselected.
  • Approves correct items by category group.
  • Supports dragging uncertain charges into category buckets.

Corrections create personal merchant-to-category preferences that take priority in future suggestions. Claude may still ask when a merchant is ambiguous, but it must not silently categorize transactions. Each submitted batch records whether the category came from AI suggestion, remembered preference, drag-and-drop, or manual selection.

Hard month-close gate

A month cannot close while any transaction dated within that month is uncategorized. Attempting to close redirects the user to this assistant with the unresolved charges loaded. The assistant must never hide, ignore, or automatically approve them to satisfy the gate.

08 / Refund reconciliation

Reverse budget impact without deleting financial history

A refund links to its original purchase and restores the refunded amount to that purchase's category. Both records remain visible so the bank history, timing, and audit trail remain truthful.

Full refund

A `$120` refund linked to a `$120` purchase produces zero net envelope impact. The original charge and refund remain as linked records rather than being erased.

Partial refund

A `$40` refund linked to a `$120` purchase restores `$40` to the original envelope and leaves `$80` of net spending.

Users may manually enter an expected refund before it appears in the connected account. It must be visibly marked as manual and pending. When the provider later supplies the real refund, the app should offer to match it to the manual record rather than count both.

Refunds may arrive after the purchase month has closed. Linking or correcting one reopens the affected historical calculation and automatically recalculates envelope balances through every later month, with an audit entry recording the before-and-after effect.

Spread transactions need explicit reversal rules

When a refund applies to a transaction whose budget impact was spread across months, the system must reverse completed and future installments proportionally to the refunded amount. The prototype should show this calculation before applying it.

09 / Interactive notifications

Suggest first, then ask for confirmation

New charge · A7 Chipotle · $18.42 Suggested: Dining Reply Y to confirm or M for categories.
Y
Added $18.42 to Dining. Available: $85.33 You are at 72% of this envelope.

Charges are handled one conversation at a time. Additional incoming transactions wait in a queue until the active charge is categorized or deferred. Unanswered charges remain uncategorized and visible in the web inbox.

Supported interaction goals include:

  • Confirm the suggested category.
  • Request a numbered category menu.
  • Ask for a category balance.
  • Ask for a monthly summary.
  • Add a manual charge.
  • Confirm or reject likely duplicates.

Near-limit and over-limit warnings should be included in categorization replies. Proactive warning delivery remains a configurable implementation decision.

SMS billing, provider selection, sender registration, and usage limits remain deferred. The prototype must simulate the complete conversation without requiring those commercial decisions.

10 / Dashboard, profile, and security

Show what changed, then protect the controls

After each successful login, the summary page compares current activity with the user's prior successful login. It displays:

New charges detectedUncategorized chargesRefunds received

Each count opens its filtered list. The refunds list shows the original purchase, refunded amount, destination envelope, affected month, and resulting envelope change.

Profile controls

  • Change username after reauthentication.
  • Change password after verifying the current password.
  • Recover forgotten credentials through a verified recovery channel.
  • Export transactions, categories, balances, and monthly history as CSV.

Connected-account controls

  • Add a credit-card connection through the bank-data provider.
  • Edit card nicknames and whether an account participates in budgeting.
  • Reconnect an expired connection without creating duplicates.
  • Remove provider access and stop future synchronization.

The app must not manage bank credentials

“Edit bank information” means managing the connection, nickname, visibility, and reconnection flow. Bank usernames, passwords, and full card numbers belong only in the provider's secure linking interface and must never be stored, displayed, or edited by this application.

The session locks after five minutes without mouse, keyboard, touch, or navigation activity. Unlocking requires authentication. Active use resets the inactivity timer; the application must warn shortly before locking and preserve unsaved review selections where safe.

11 / Prototype and MVP

Validate the behavior before paying for integrations

The first prototype remains a web application with simulated bank synchronization and a simulated SMS conversation. It validates the product model while preserving clean boundaries for real providers later.

Prototype includes

  • Guided account setup.
  • Category templates.
  • 50/30/20 or custom allocation.
  • Editable envelope targets.
  • Simulated incoming bank charges.
  • AI-like merchant cleanup with corrections.
  • Claude-style batch categorization assistant.
  • Conversation plus drag-and-drop review workspace.
  • Interactive SMS simulator.
  • Since-last-login activity summary.
  • Category summary and transaction details.
  • Uncategorized inbox.
  • Month-close gate and rollover.
  • Spread budget impact.
  • Full and partial refund reconciliation.
  • Profile and simulated connection management.
  • Audit history and warnings.

Deferred from prototype

  • Live bank aggregator.
  • Live SMS provider and phone number.
  • CSV card exports.
  • Spreadsheet migration.
  • Split transactions.
  • Checking and savings accounts.
  • Shared household budgets.
  • Native mobile applications.

The production architecture should isolate bank connectivity, SMS delivery, Claude access, merchant normalization, and budgeting rules behind separate interfaces. This allows simulated providers to be replaced without rewriting the budgeting product.

11B / Public Launch MVP (September 16, 2026)

Validate core product with real users before feature polish

The first public release focuses on multi-user functionality and core workflows. It supports 5–7 simultaneous test users and allows early customer testing. Deferred items are noted for post-launch.

Public launch includes

  • User signup and account creation.
  • Persistent per-user profiles and data storage.
  • Secure login with session management.
  • Public URL and HTTPS deployment.
  • Existing onboarding (category setup, 50/30/20).
  • Category and envelope management.
  • Simulated bank charge entry (manual UI).
  • Transaction categorization (single charge + AI batch review).
  • Month-close and rollover behavior.
  • Available balance tracking.
  • Refund reconciliation.
  • SMS simulator (user interaction, no real SMS yet).
  • Basic audit history and warnings.

Deferred from public launch

  • Onboarding coach / feature education GIFs.
  • Bulk automatic charge simulation.
  • Live bank aggregator (Plaid or equivalent).
  • Real Twilio SMS integration (requires public URLs for compliance).
  • Password reset / email recovery.
  • Email verification on signup.
  • CSV export.
  • Spread spending feature.
  • Advanced session timeout enforcement.

Target user base: 5 internal test accounts + 2 early customer accounts. Designed to collect feedback on core workflows without requiring full feature parity.

Data storage: SQLite with user isolation. Each user sees only their own profiles, transactions, and categories. No shared or household budgets in this version.

Success criteria: Users can create accounts, set up budgets, categorize transactions, close months, and see accurate envelope balances across multiple months.

12 / Hosting and production decisions

The operating posture is decided; providers remain open

The product should be available from any phone or computer using low-maintenance managed hosting. The initial target is `$10–$30 per month` before bank-data, SMS, and Claude usage fees.

AreaAgreed direction
Managed hostingUse managed application and relational-database services with HTTPS, encrypted storage, managed secrets, health monitoring, and minimal server maintenance.
BackupsCreate daily protected database backups and retain them for 30 days. Restoration must be tested before production launch.
Data exportAllow CSV export of transactions, categories, envelope balances, and monthly history.
Account deletionRevoke bank access and disable login immediately. Hold user data for a 30-day recovery window, then permanently delete it from active systems according to a documented backup-expiration process.
Claude privacySend only minimum necessary merchant and category context. Exclude identity, card details, bank identifiers, credentials, and unnecessary raw metadata.
Session securityLock after five minutes of inactivity and require authentication to resume.

Still to be selected before production

  • Bank-data provider: compare coverage, update timing, pending reconciliation, OAuth behavior, price, and production approval.
  • SMS provider: compare United States registration, consent and opt-out handling, sender type, throughput, and cost.
  • Managed host: compare actual all-in pricing against the budget after database, backups, monitoring, and outbound networking are included.
  • Claude commercial configuration: verify API data handling and retention terms, model cost, rate limits, and failure fallback.
  • Pending-to-posted reconciliation: define behavior when amount or merchant details change before posting.

Connection failure behavior already decided

If bank synchronization stops, notify the user by text, show a persistent dashboard warning, support reconnection without duplicates, and permit manual entry during the gap.

13 / Prototype acceptance criteria

Evidence required before calling the prototype successful

  1. A new user can choose a starter template and a 50/30/20 or custom allocation without beginning from an empty category screen.
  2. The setup calculates suggested category amounts from the monthly budgeting total and allows every amount to be changed.
  3. A simulated connected card can produce a new transaction containing amount, date, account nickname, and raw description.
  4. The product displays an editable cleaned merchant name while preserving the original bank description in transaction detail.
  5. The simulated SMS experience suggests a category and accepts confirmation or a numbered alternate selection.
  6. Unanswered transactions remain in an uncategorized inbox and do not disappear.
  7. The AI review assistant summarizes uncategorized charges, proposes category groups, lets users deselect mistakes, and submits accepted groups together.
  8. Uncertain charges can be assigned through assistant questions, manual controls, or drag-and-drop category buckets.
  9. A corrected merchant-category pairing influences later suggestions without silently categorizing the charge.
  10. Categorizing a transaction updates the envelope and displays near-limit or over-limit status.
  11. Month closure is blocked while dated charges remain uncategorized and redirects directly to the AI review workspace.
  12. After all charges are resolved, month closure adds each active category's contribution and carries positive and negative balances forward.
  13. Historical transaction corrections recalculate affected envelope balances through later months and retain an audit record.
  14. Full and partial refunds link to the original purchase, preserve both records, and restore the correct amount to the original envelope.
  15. A manually entered expected refund is not double-counted when its connected-bank record later arrives.
  16. The summary page reports new charges, uncategorized charges, and refunds since the prior successful login, with drill-down lists.
  17. The profile supports credential changes and simulated add, rename, reconnect, disable, and remove operations for connected cards without handling bank credentials.
  18. The application locks after five minutes of inactivity and requires authentication to resume.
  19. A `$600` transaction spread across six months remains one `$600` bank charge while showing `$100` of budget impact in the purchase month and five later months.
  20. Changing a spread duration recalculates its remaining monthly impacts without modifying the original bank transaction.
  21. The interface distinguishes actual card charges from smoothed budget impact wherever the two totals differ.

Approved direction: connected data, guided setup, deliberate categorization.

This definition supersedes the earlier forwarded-bank-text workflow. No implementation should begin by assuming the original SMS source. The prototype should model bank synchronization and interactive app-originated texts, while real providers remain replaceable integration decisions.
